## Overview

By now this platform has covered three genuinely different ways to change
an LLM's behavior: prompt engineering (change the instructions), RAG
(change what information it has access to), and fine-tuning (change the
model's actual weights). They aren't interchangeable, and reaching for
fine-tuning first is one of the most common expensive mistakes teams make.

## The decision order, and why it's ordered this way

```
1. Prompt engineering  -- cheapest, fastest to iterate, zero infra
2. RAG                 -- adds retrieval infra, but no training needed
3. Fine-tuning          -- needs a training pipeline, data, compute, evaluation
```

Each step up costs meaningfully more in engineering complexity and
iteration speed. A prompt change is testable in seconds; a RAG pipeline
change might take an afternoon to properly re-evaluate; a fine-tuning run
takes hours-to-days and needs a real training/eval pipeline (next module)
around it. The practical implication: exhaust the cheaper options first,
and only fine-tune once you've identified a *specific* gap that prompting
and retrieval genuinely can't close.

## What fine-tuning is actually good for

- **Consistent style/format at scale** — if you need a very specific
  output format followed with extremely high reliability across huge
  volumes of requests, fine-tuning on many examples of that exact format
  can be more reliable than relying on few-shot examples in every single
  prompt (which also cost context budget every time, per the context
  engineering lesson).
- **Domain-specific language patterns** — legal, medical, or highly
  technical domains with vocabulary and conventions underrepresented in
  general training data.
- **Distillation** — training a smaller, cheaper model to imitate a
  larger model's outputs on your specific task, trading some general
  capability for much lower cost per request.

## What fine-tuning is a poor fit for

- **Adding new factual knowledge** — RAG is almost always the better tool
  here: retrieval can be updated instantly by changing the document store,
  while fine-tuned-in facts are frozen again the moment training ends, and
  fine-tuning doesn't reliably teach a model new facts the way people
  often assume — it's better understood as adjusting *behavior* than
  injecting *knowledge*.
- **A problem you haven't tried prompting/RAG on yet** — without first
  establishing what those cheaper approaches can't fix, there's no way to
  know fine-tuning is actually the missing piece rather than a prompt or
  retrieval issue in disguise.
