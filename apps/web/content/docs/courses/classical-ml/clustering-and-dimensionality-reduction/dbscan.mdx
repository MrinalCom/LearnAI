## Overview

K-means and hierarchical clustering both rely fundamentally on **distance to
a center point**. **DBSCAN** (Density-Based Spatial Clustering of
Applications with Noise) takes a completely different approach: it groups
together points that are packed closely, and marks points in low-density
regions as **noise** rather than forcing them into a cluster.

This directly solves two problems from the previous two lessons: irregular
(non-spherical) cluster shapes, and datasets that genuinely contain
outliers that shouldn't belong to any cluster at all.

## How it works

DBSCAN has two parameters: `eps` (a distance radius) and `min_samples` (a
count). It classifies every point as one of three types:

- **Core point** — has at least `min_samples` other points within `eps` of
  it.
- **Border point** — within `eps` of a core point, but doesn't itself have
  enough neighbors to be core.
- **Noise point** — neither. Not assigned to any cluster.

A cluster is formed by taking a core point, absorbing every point reachable
from it through a chain of neighboring core points ("density-reachability"),
plus their border points. This is why DBSCAN can trace out clusters of
arbitrary shape — like the ring-around-a-center-cluster case from the SVM
lesson — that k-means, which only understands "distance to one center,"
cannot.

## In real code

<PyodidePlayground initialCode={`import numpy as np
from sklearn.cluster import DBSCAN

# A dense ring surrounding a dense center cluster, plus a couple of far-flung outliers.
rng = np.random.RandomState(0)
angle = rng.uniform(0, 2 * np.pi, size=30)
ring = np.column_stack([4 * np.cos(angle), 4 * np.sin(angle)]) + rng.normal(0, 0.2, size=(30, 2))
center = rng.normal(0, 0.4, size=(20, 2))
outliers = np.array([[10, 10], [-10, -8]])

X = np.vstack([ring, center, outliers])

model = DBSCAN(eps=0.8, min_samples=4)
labels = model.fit_predict(X)

n_clusters = len(set(labels)) - (1 if -1 in labels else 0)
n_noise = list(labels).count(-1)
print(f"Found {n_clusters} clusters and {n_noise} noise points")
print("Labels for the two outliers:", labels[-2:])
`}
/>

Noise points are labeled `-1`. Notice you never told DBSCAN how many
clusters to look for — unlike k-means and (in practice) hierarchical
clustering, the number of clusters is discovered automatically from the
density structure of the data.

## Choosing eps and min_samples

There's no `k` to pick, but `eps` and `min_samples` matter just as much:

- **`eps` too small** — most points become their own noise cluster.
- **`eps` too large** — everything merges into one giant cluster.
- **`min_samples`** — higher values make the algorithm more conservative
  about calling something a core point, which is more robust to noise but
  can miss small, genuine clusters.

A common approach is to plot the distance to each point's k-th nearest
neighbor (sorted) and look for a similar "elbow" to the one used for
choosing `k` in k-means.
